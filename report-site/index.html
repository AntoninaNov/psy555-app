The idea

Knowledge is a map, not a list

Tolman (1948) showed rats build internal maps of mazes; O'Keefe and Nadel (1978) located the substrate in the hippocampus. The same topology generalises to semantic memory — networks of related ideas obey the laws Barabási and Albert (1999) and Latora and Marchiori (2001) described for physical networks. What makes someone an expert isn't more facts. It's how those facts connect (Chase & Simon, 1973; Chi, Feltovich, & Glaser, 1981) — bounded by working-memory limits (Miller, 1956; Sweller, 1988; Kahneman, 2011).

GPA averages exam outputs. It cannot see the topology underneath. We wanted to.

Spatial-navigation circuits, ancient in mammals, are repurposed for abstract reasoning.

Energy-efficient topologies win when metabolic cost matters — at exam time, ranking matters more (Geary, 2008; Nesse & Williams, 1994).

The protocol

One hour. Five blocks.

Each session ran the same five-block sequence on the study app. Full session traces are in the public JSON.

A

Cluster

Group the 14 ILOs into 4 thematic clusters; pick a "central" ILO per cluster.

B

Map

Connect any two ILOs you see related. Weight every edge w ∈ {1, 2, 3}.

C

Self-rate

Rate your own competence on each ILO on a Likert 1–7 scale.

D

Pathfinding

Given two ILOs, choose the shortest weighted path on your own graph.

E

Perturbation

One ILO is removed. Pick a structural replacement among the rest.

What we found

Three patterns GPA never sees

⚠ The ceiling came first

Nine of eleven students saturated density = 1.000. With near-zero variance, the four GPA-anchored probes we wanted to run on density, hierarchy, and weight differentiation aren't resolvable in this sample. That artefact is itself the first finding — and the design fix is an edge budget, a forced ranking, and a precision-weighted score. Three patterns still emerged from the residual variance.

1. Higher map integration → lower self-rating (the Socratic effect)

ρ = −0.69, pperm = .023 Students whose maps look more integrated rate their own competence lower. The effect persists with the outlier removed (ρ = −0.58, N = 10) — the textbook Socratic pattern (Flavell, 1979; Eva & Regehr, 2005).

2. More confident → slower under perturbation

ρ = +0.77, pperm = .008 Students with higher self-ratings take longer per perturbation choice, not shorter — most plausibly task investment. The single Dunning–Kruger case (next section) is off-trend in the opposite direction.

3. All eleven students weight within-cluster ties higher

Without exception. Δ from 0.20 (S6) to 0.90 (S5). Even the outlier with 17 edges shows Δ = 0.36. The unanimity matters: it confirms that the prior thematic clustering of the 14 ILOs is recovered by participants without prompting — ground truth for cluster validity at scale.

The single case

One participant. Nine indicators. A textbook profile.

One participant (S7, GPA = 93) deviates from the rest of the sample on nine indicators in a coherent direction — peak self-evaluation co-occurs with minimum structural competence. That's nine simultaneous deviations from the median in the predicted direction (Dunning & Kruger, 1999).

The full study lands next academic year

This pilot answered one question: does the instrument produce a usable signal? It does. Three large effects in N = 11 — and a clear design fix for the ceiling. Next iteration: 300+ students across the undergraduate Psychology programme, edge-budget cap, forced-ranking weights, precision-weighted scoring.

N ≈ 300+ Edge budget ≤ 30 Forced ranking Precision penalty Pre-registered

Curriculum-design diagnostic at programme scale. Email the authors if you want to run the same protocol on your cohort.

⚠ Read every effect through these

N = 11 — critical |ρ| at α = .05 is ≈ 0.62. Density ceiling — four probes not resolvable. No external knowledge criterion — GPA is a grade proxy, not a knowledge measure. Single cohort, single institution. Exploratory throughout — every effect listed here is hypothesis-generating for the next study.
